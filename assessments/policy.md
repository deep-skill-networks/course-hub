# Assessment and certification policy

Assessments here exist mainly to help you learn. Retrieval practice and feedback are some of the most reliable ways to make knowledge stick, so most quizzes allow two attempts and every answer comes with an explanation.

## Cohort (Live + Self-paced)

| Component | When | Weight | How it's assessed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diagnostic pre-test | Before week 1 | 0% | 30 questions; repeated at the end to measure your progress |
| Live session check-ins | Every session | 0% | Short polls, for participation and feedback |
| Weekly quizzes | Weekly | 15% | 10–12 scenario questions, two attempts, including three review items from earlier modules |
| Labs | Weekly | 20% | Autograded; pass/fail with credit for stretch goals |
| Break-fix labs and incident replays | ~6 across the course | 15% | Rubric: scoping, hypotheses, isolation, fix quality, prevention |
| Design reviews | M03, M05, M08 | 15% | Rubric: framing, requirements, soundness, failure modes, operations plan, trade-offs |
| Capstone milestones `ms1`–`ms7` | Every two weeks | 15% | Checklist plus mentor review |
| Game day and capstone defense | M08 | 20% | Rubric covering architecture, evals, production readiness, detection, incident handling and client communication |

**Certificate:** a weighted score of at least 70%, all milestones submitted, and the capstone defended.
**With distinction:** at least 85%, and you detected the hardest game-day faults through your own monitoring.

## Fully self-paced (each of SP1–SP4)

- At least 70% across quizzes
- Every lab passed
- A final practical: one autograded lab plus one incident-replay diagnosis, scored against a rubric

Completing all four courses earns the program certificate.

## Deadlines and extensions (cohort)
- Milestones are due at the end of their module's second week.
- If you're going to miss one, tell the mentor before the deadline. One no-questions-asked extension of one week is available per learner.
- If you miss a milestone or two sessions in a row, the mentor will get in touch. That's support, not a penalty.

## Using AI tools
You're training to work alongside AI tools, so using them is allowed and expected: coding agents, chat assistants, anything. The rules:
1. **You must understand and be able to explain everything you submit.** Design reviews and the defense will test this.
2. **Disclose significant AI help** in your pull request description (one line is enough).
3. **Incident diagnoses must show your reasoning**, not just the answer.

## Academic honesty
- Don't share lab solutions or game-day details outside your cohort.
- Discussing approaches with your pod is encouraged. Submitting someone else's work as your own is not.
- Breaches may lead to loss of credit for the work and, if repeated, removal from the program.
